5 Days in Budapest: Danube, Castle Hill & Thermal Baths

From the Parliament's riverside glow on the Pest side to the thermal waters of Széchenyi and the cobbled lanes of Castle Hill, five days is enough time to cross the Danube, soak in Budapest's most famous bathhouse, and still catch a sunset cruise before you leave.

Ideal SeasonApr – Jun, Sep – Oct
Est. Budget$$ · ~$700
Duration5 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & transfer into Pest

Budapest Ferenc Liszt International Airport sits about 16 km southeast of the center, and the 100E direct bus drops you at Deák Ferenc tér in under an hour.

🚌 ~40 min on the 100E direct bus 💵 ~€6 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Afternoon

Danube promenade & the Parliament building

Walk the Pest-side promenade from the Chain Bridge north past the Shoes on the Danube Bank memorial to the Hungarian Parliament, its neo-Gothic spires best seen from directly across the river.

🚶 Along the riverside from Deák Ferenc tér 💵 Free to walk the promenade 🕐 Golden hour, about an hour before sunset
Evening

First ruin bar in the Jewish Quarter

Budapest invented the ruin bar, a crumbling courtyard or apartment building filled with mismatched furniture and string lights, and Szimpla Kert in District VII is the original.

🚶 ~15 min walk to District VII 💵 ~€4 per drink 🕐 After 8 PM, once the courtyard fills in
Morning

Funicular up to Fisherman's Bastion

The Budavári Sikló funicular climbs from Clark Ádám tér to Castle Hill in a couple of minutes, and reaching Fisherman's Bastion's neo-Romanesque turrets right at opening means clear photos before the tour buses arrive.

🚊 Budavári Sikló funicular from Clark Ádám tér 💵 ~€5 funicular, ~€4 upper terrace 🕐 Right at 9 AM opening, before tour groups
Midday

Buda Castle grounds

The former royal palace now houses the Hungarian National Gallery and the Budapest History Museum behind its courtyards, worth a slow lap even if you skip the museums themselves.

🚶 A few minutes' walk from Fisherman's Bastion 💵 Free courtyards, ~€13 gallery entry 🕐 Late morning, once the funicular queue thins
Afternoon

Matthias Church

The coronation church of Hungarian kings, with a diamond-patterned roof and a Habsburg crypt, sits a short walk from the Bastion at the heart of the Castle District.

🚶 Steps from Fisherman's Bastion 💵 ~€6 entry 🕐 Mid-afternoon, or check for an evening organ concert
Morning

Széchenyi Thermal Baths

Europe's largest bathing complex has three outdoor pools and eighteen indoor ones, all fed by a natural thermal spring, and arriving at opening means claiming a spot in the yellow Neo-Baroque courtyard before the tour groups.

🚇 M1 to Széchenyi fürdő station 💵 ~€22 day ticket with locker 🕐 Right at 9 AM opening
Afternoon

Poolside lunch & more soaking

Stay in and around the baths through lunch, there's a poolside snack bar for something light, then linger in the outdoor pools where locals play chess on floating boards.

🚶 Inside the Széchenyi complex 💵 ~€10 lunch 🕐 Early afternoon, the quieter stretch before weekend crowds peak
Evening

City Park & Heroes' Square

Walk out of the baths into Városliget, past Vajdahunyad Castle's mismatched architectural styles, to Heroes' Square and its Millennium Monument, striking in the low evening light.

🚶 ~10 min walk through City Park 💵 Free to visit the square 🕐 Golden hour, before sunset
Morning

Great Market Hall

Budapest's largest indoor market fills three levels with paprika stalls and butchers, plus a food court upstairs serving lángos, a fried dough classic worth queueing for.

🚇 M4 or tram 47/49 to Fővám tér 💵 ~€6 lángos and market snacks 🕐 Mid-morning, once the stalls are fully open
Afternoon

Jewish Quarter & Dohány Street Synagogue

Europe's largest synagogue anchors District VII, its twin towers and Moorish Revival interior backing onto the Tree of Life memorial in the courtyard behind it.

🚶 ~15 min walk from the Great Market Hall 💵 ~€16 synagogue and museum entry 🕐 Early afternoon, before the last entry cutoff
Evening

Danube river cruise

A sunset cruise past the Chain Bridge, Buda Castle, and the illuminated Parliament building is the best way to see everything from Days 1 and 2 lit up at once.

⛴️ Boarding near Vigadó tér or Batthyány tér 💵 ~€20 per person 🕐 Sunset departure, about an hour before dark
Morning

Margaret Island

This car-free island in the middle of the Danube is where locals go to slow down, with rose gardens, a musical fountain, and a running track that circles the whole park.

🚋 Tram 4 or 6 to Margit-sziget 💵 Free to walk the island 🕐 Mid-morning, before the day's heat sets in
Midday

Last-minute shopping on Váci utca

Budapest's main pedestrian shopping street runs from Vörösmarty tér toward the Great Market Hall, with everything from souvenir paprika tins to Herend porcelain.

🚶 M1 to Vörösmarty tér 💵 Budget to taste 🕐 Early afternoon, before shops close for the evening
Afternoon

Transfer to the airport

The 100E bus runs back to Ferenc Liszt International in about 40 minutes, or book a licensed Főtaxi or Bolt car if you're traveling with luggage.

🚌 ~40 min on the 100E direct bus 💵 ~€6 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Around 10% is standard at sit-down restaurants, but check the bill first, since some places already add a service charge.
  • Card machines rarely have a tip line, so you'll usually need to say the total including tip out loud when you pay, or leave the tip in cash.
  • At ruin bars and casual cafes, rounding up the bill is enough, there's no real obligation on a simple bar tab.

👗 Dress Code

  • The Dohány Street Synagogue and Matthias Church both expect shoulders and knees covered on entry.
  • Bring a modest cover-up beyond your swimwear for Széchenyi Baths, since you'll walk between pools, changing rooms, and the courtyard in it.
  • Smart-casual works for the evening river cruise and ruin bars, and solid shoes matter most on Day 2, given Castle Hill's cobblestones.

🚇 Getting Around Budapest

  • The BKK network covers four metro lines (M1, continental Europe's oldest underground, among them), trams, and buses on a single ticket system.
  • A single ticket costs around €1.10, or a 24-hour travel pass at about €6.50 comfortably covers this itinerary's busiest transit days.
  • Validate paper tickets at the small machines before boarding trams and buses; metro turnstiles validate automatically as you pass through.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Unmarked "taxis" waiting right outside the airport arrivals hall and near Parliament are the most common overcharging scam, insist on a metered, licensed yellow Főtaxi or book through the Bolt app instead.
  • Some card machines at bars near Váci utca and District VII quietly apply a bad exchange rate through dynamic currency conversion, always choose to pay in HUF, not your home currency.
  • Pickpockets work the crowded M2 line and the tourist-heavy stretches near the Chain Bridge, keep bags zipped and to the front.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Hungarian

  • Szia: hi (informal)
  • Köszönöm szépen: thank you very much
  • A számlát legyen szíves: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • Hungary uses the forint (HUF), not the euro, despite being an EU member, and some tourist spots quote euro prices at a worse rate than paying in HUF.
  • Contactless cards are accepted almost everywhere, including metro ticket machines and Great Market Hall stalls.
  • Carry some HUF cash for smaller market stalls and for topping up transit cards at machines that don't take contactless.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🌙

Evening ticket discount at Széchenyi

Entry booked after 7 PM costs noticeably less than a full-day ticket, and the courtyard is lit up and less crowded after dark.

🍲

Order the "napi menü"

Local bistros post a daily set lunch menu, a starter and main for a fraction of what the same dishes cost off the evening menu.

🎟️

Buy a 24-hour BKK pass

If you're taking more than four metro, tram, or bus rides in a day, the day pass beats paying per ride almost every time.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🍸

Instant-Fogas Complex over Szimpla's crowds

A sprawling multi-room ruin bar complex a few streets from Szimpla Kert, with the same courtyard atmosphere and far shorter lines at the door.

♨️

Rudas Baths as a quieter thermal alternative

An Ottoman-era bathhouse on the Buda side with an octagonal domed pool, calmer and more local than Széchenyi's tourist crowds.

🌄

Gellért Hill at sunrise

The Citadella viewpoint above the Danube is nearly empty before 8 AM, a completely different mood from its busy midday tour-bus crowds.

✈️ Flights

  • Budapest Ferenc Liszt International Airport (BUD) is the only major gateway, about 16 km southeast of the center.
  • The 100E direct bus reaches Deák Ferenc tér in around 40 minutes for a fraction of a taxi fare.
  • Bus 200E connecting to the M3 metro is a slower but cheaper alternative if you're not in a hurry.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Bolt operates widely across Budapest and is the easiest way to book a fairly priced ride from a phone.
  • Avoid unmarked drivers who approach you inside the airport arrivals hall, this is the city's best-known overcharging scam.
  • If you flag a street taxi, confirm it's a yellow, licensed Főtaxi with a visible meter before getting in.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not needed for this itinerary, every stop from the Parliament to Castle Hill sits on the BKK metro, tram, or walking network.
  • Castle Hill and the Jewish Quarter both have limited, expensive parking that makes driving more hassle than it's worth.
  • Skip the rental and put the budget toward baths tickets and the river cruise instead.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Inner Pest, around Deák Ferenc tér: puts the Day 1 Danube promenade and evening ruin bars within walking distance.
  • District VII, the Jewish Quarter: closest to Day 1's bar scene and Day 4's synagogue, with plenty of nightlife right outside.
  • Watertown (Víziváros), below Castle Hill: a quieter Buda base with an easy funicular or bus ride up to Day 2's sights.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • The Danube river cruise on Day 4 is a genuine highlight here, not a filler activity, and the best way to see the Parliament and Castle Hill lit up at night.
  • Book a sunset departure rather than a daytime one for the best light on the buildings.
  • Longer dinner cruises are available, but a standard hour-long sightseeing cruise covers the same views for less.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Hungary is in the Schengen Area, so standard Schengen entry rules apply for non-EU visitors.
  • Remember the currency is the Hungarian forint (HUF), not the euro, even though Hungary is an EU member.
  • Travel insurance is worth it given the pre-booked Széchenyi and synagogue tickets, which are usually non-refundable.
