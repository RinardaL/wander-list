3 Days in Hakone: Mount Fuji Views, Onsen & Lake Ashi

A red torii gate rising straight out of Lake Ashi, a steaming volcanic valley crossed by cable car, and a ryokan soak with Mount Fuji watching over it all. Three unhurried days in Japan's classic hot-spring escape, less than two hours from Tokyo.

Ideal SeasonOct – Feb
Est. Budget$750-1,050
Duration3 Days
PaceRelaxed to Moderate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Odakyu Romancecar from Shinjuku to Hakone-Yumoto

The limited-express train is the easiest way in: no transfers, reserved seats, and the mountains close in as Tokyo falls away behind you.

🚄 ~85 min Romancecar 💵 ~$25 🕐 Morning departure
Afternoon

Hakone Open-Air Museum

More than 100 sculptures scattered across a hillside park, plus an entire pavilion of Picasso ceramics and paintings.

🚃 one stop on the Hakone Tozan Railway 💵 ~$14 🕐 Allow 2-3 hours
Evening

Check into a ryokan & a first onsen soak

A traditional inn with its own hot-spring bath is the whole point of Hakone: sink in before the kaiseki dinner arrives.

🚕 short taxi to most ryokans 💵 Included in stay 🕐 Before dinner, ~6 PM
Morning

Hakone Ropeway over Owakudani

A cable car ride above a still-active volcanic valley, with sulfur vents hissing through the rock far below.

🚡 ~30 min ropeway, via a cable car transfer at Sounzan 💵 ~$25 round trip 🕐 Morning, clearest air for Fuji views
Afternoon

Black eggs at Owakudani, then a cruise across Lake Ashi

Local kuro-tamago are boiled in the hot springs until the shells turn black; tradition says each one adds seven years to your life. Ride the ropeway down to Togendai afterward for a sightseeing cruise across the lake, some boats styled like pirate ships.

⛴️ ~30 min lake cruise 💵 ~$4 for eggs, ~$10 cruise 🕐 Early afternoon crossing
Evening

Hakone Shrine's torii gate at Motohakone

The red torii gate standing right in the water is the postcard shot of the whole trip, quieter and more atmospheric as the light fades.

🚶 lakeside path from the pier 💵 Free 🕐 Late afternoon, before sunset
Morning

A slow morning soak in the ryokan onsen

No agenda, just hot mineral water and, on a clear day, a view toward Mount Fuji from the bath itself.

🚶 on-site at the ryokan 💵 Included in stay 🕐 Before checkout
Afternoon

A walk on the old Tokaido highway

A short stretch of the Hakone Old Road still runs beneath towering Edo-period cedars, planted centuries ago to shade feudal-era travelers.

🚶 ~30 min easy walk 💵 Free 🕐 Late morning
Evening

Return to Tokyo

Retrace the route back to Hakone-Yumoto and board the Romancecar for the ride back to Shinjuku.

🚄 ~85 min Romancecar 💵 ~$25 🕐 Afternoon departure, allow buffer

💵 Tipping Etiquette

  • Tipping isn't customary in Japan and can even cause confusion; attentive service is the norm, not something extra to pay for.
  • Never leave cash on a table. If a ryokan or restaurant adds a service charge, it's already included in the bill.
  • Ryokan staff won't expect a tip even after especially attentive care.

♨️ Onsen Etiquette

  • Wash and rinse thoroughly at the shower stations before entering the communal bath; soap never touches the onsen water itself.
  • Many traditional onsens still turn away visible tattoos, so ask your ryokan in advance if this applies to you.
  • Swimsuits aren't worn in the bath; a small modesty towel stays out of the water, usually resting on your head.

🚋 Getting Around

  • The Hakone Free Pass covers the Tozan Railway, the cable car, the ropeway, and the Lake Ashi cruise, well worth it for the Round Course alone.
  • The trains, cable car, and ropeway run along one fixed loop, so plan the day in a single direction rather than backtracking.
  • Coin lockers at Hakone-Yumoto Station make it easy to explore without dragging luggage around all day.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Owakudani's sulfur vents occasionally trigger volcanic activity alerts that close the ropeway's upper section, so check current status before heading up.
  • The valley's rotten-egg smell is normal sulfur gas, but anyone with respiratory issues should keep visits brief.
  • Lake Ashi cruises can be cancelled in high wind; have a backup plan for the afternoon just in case.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Japanese

  • Ohayou gozaimasu: good morning
  • Arigatou gozaimasu: thank you
  • Onsen wa doko desu ka? where is the hot spring?

💳 Money & Payments

  • Japanese Yen (JPY) cash is still preferred at smaller ryokans and food stalls, though cards are increasingly accepted.
  • IC cards like Suica or Pasmo work on the Hakone Tozan Railway and local buses, handy for quick taps.
  • 7-Eleven and post office ATMs are the most reliable options for withdrawing cash on a foreign card.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎫

Buy the Hakone Free Pass

It covers nearly every train, cable car, ropeway ride, and the Lake Ashi cruise on one ticket, cheaper than paying leg by leg.

🧖

Skip the extra onsen towel

Most ryokans include a modesty towel with the room, no need to buy a second one at Owakudani.

🍱

Let the ryokan feed you

A kaiseki dinner is usually included in the stay, so save eating out for casual lunches at station soba stands instead.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌲

Walk the full Hakone Old Road

The cedar-lined Tokaido stretch sees a fraction of the crowds found at Owakudani or the torii gate.

🏛️

The museum's smaller pavilions

Beyond the Picasso hall, the Symphonic Sculpture tower's stained-glass interior rarely gets the attention it deserves.

⛩️

The torii gate at first light

Arrive before the cruise boats start running for a nearly empty shot of the shrine gate standing in the lake.

✈️ Flights

  • Tokyo's Narita (NRT) or Haneda (HND) airports are the gateways; Haneda sits closer to Shinjuku, where the Hakone-bound Romancecar departs.
  • Budget at least 90 minutes to clear immigration and reach Shinjuku Station by train or airport limousine bus.
  • No airport serves Hakone directly, this trip runs as a rail journey out of central Tokyo rather than its own flight leg.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Taxis wait outside Hakone-Yumoto Station, and most ryokans will arrange one for the final stretch up to your room.
  • Uber has limited coverage in Hakone; local taxi-hailing apps like GO are more reliable.
  • A short taxi ride often beats waiting for an infrequent local bus after dark.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • Not necessary and often more hassle than it's worth: the Hakone Free Pass covers the entire Round Course by rail, cable car, ropeway, and boat.
  • Roads around Owakudani and the lake get congested on weekends and during autumn foliage season.
  • If self-driving in from Tokyo, allow extra time for the mountain switchbacks near Gora and Sounzan.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Gora or Hakone-Yumoto: classic ryokan territory, both sitting directly on the Hakone Tozan Railway line.
  • Motohakone or Hakone-machi: lakeside, closest to the torii gate and the sightseeing cruise piers.
  • Book a ryokan with a private or reservable onsen well ahead for weekend and autumn-foliage dates.

🚋 Trains & Cable Cars

  • The Hakone Tozan Railway's switchback climb, the Sounzan-Owakudani cable car, and the Hakone Ropeway together form the backbone of the Round Course.
  • The Hakone Free Pass covers all three plus the Lake Ashi cruise; buy it at Shinjuku Station before leaving Tokyo.
  • Check ropeway status before heading up, volcanic activity occasionally suspends the Owakudani section.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Most nationalities get visa-free entry to Japan for short tourist stays; confirm the current rule for your passport before flying.
  • Travel insurance is worth carrying for gear left in ryokan storage and for weather-related transport delays.
  • Pack for cooler mountain temperatures even in shoulder seasons, Hakone runs noticeably cooler than central Tokyo.
