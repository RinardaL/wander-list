4 Days in Prague: Old Town, Castle & the Vltava

Gothic spires over Old Town Square, a hilltop castle complex, a synagogue-studded Jewish Quarter, and a final panorama from Petřín Hill, four unhurried days across one of Central Europe's best-preserved cities.

Ideal SeasonApr – Jun, Sep – Oct
Est. Budget$$ · $550-750
Duration4 Days
PaceModerate
How many days do you have?

Morning

Arrival & check-in near the Old Town

Václav Havel Airport connects into the center by bus and metro in about 35 minutes, so most visitors are dropping bags well before lunch.

🚌 ~35 min bus + metro from the airport 💵 ~€1.50 🕐 Plan around your flight's landing time
Midday

Old Town Square, early and uncrowded

The square's Gothic and Baroque facades, anchored by Týn Church's twin spires, are easiest to take in before the tour groups and souvenir stalls fill the cobblestones.

🚶 Central, walkable from most Old Town hotels 💵 Free to wander 🕐 Early morning, before 9 AM, for the emptiest square
Evening

The Astronomical Clock & an Old Town evening

Watch the medieval Astronomical Clock's Walk of the Apostles on the hour, then wander the surrounding lanes for a first Czech dinner of goulash and a local pilsner.

🚶 On Old Town Square itself 💵 Free to watch, ~€12 dinner 🕐 On the hour, any time from 9 AM to 9 PM
Morning

St. Vitus Cathedral, inside Prague Castle

The castle complex opens early, and St. Vitus Cathedral's stained glass and soaring nave are best seen before the courtyards fill with day-trippers.

🚋 Tram 22 to Pražský hrad 💵 ~€13 castle circuit ticket 🕐 Right at the 9 AM opening
Afternoon

Golden Lane's tiny artisan houses

This row of miniature 16th-century cottages, once home to castle marksmen and goldsmiths, is a quick but memorable detour inside the same complex.

🚶 Within the castle grounds 💵 Included with the castle circuit ticket 🕐 Early afternoon, between cathedral and bridge
Evening

Charles Bridge at sunset

Walk down from the castle and cross the 14th-century Charles Bridge as the light turns gold on the statues and the river below, Prague's single most photographed hour.

🚶 ~15 min downhill walk from the castle 💵 Free to cross 🕐 Golden hour, roughly an hour before sunset
Morning

Josefov & the Spanish Synagogue

The Jewish Quarter's synagogues and the Old Jewish Cemetery trace centuries of Prague's Jewish history, and the Spanish Synagogue's Moorish interior is the most striking stop on the circuit.

🚶 ~10 min walk from Old Town Square 💵 ~€15 Jewish Museum combined ticket 🕐 Mid-morning, right after opening
Midday

Vltava river cruise

A relaxed hour on the water gives a completely different view of Charles Bridge, the castle hill, and the Old Town waterfront, a nice change of pace after a morning on foot.

🚤 Boats depart from the Old Town riverbank 💵 ~€18 for a 1-hour cruise 🕐 Early afternoon, when the light is clearest on the water
Evening

Lesser Town (Malá Strana)

Cross into the quieter, palace-lined streets below the castle for dinner in a courtyard tavern, a neighborhood with far fewer crowds than the Old Town after dark.

🚶 Across Charles Bridge or the Legion Bridge 💵 ~€14 dinner 🕐 After 7 PM, once the day-trip crowds thin out
Morning

Petřín Hill & its lookout tower

A short funicular ride up Petřín Hill leads to a miniature Eiffel-style lookout tower, with the whole red-roofed city laid out below on a clear morning.

🚡 Funicular from Újezd 💵 ~€4 funicular, ~€4 tower entry 🕐 Mid-morning, before midday haze sets in
Afternoon

Last-minute shopping in the Old Town

Head back down for garnet jewelry, Bohemian crystal, and wooden toys along Old Town's shopping streets, easy to combine with a final coffee near the square.

🚶 Back across the river to Old Town 💵 Varies, ~€25 typical souvenir budget 🕐 Early afternoon, ahead of departure
Evening

Transfer to Václav Havel Airport for departure

The same bus-and-metro combination that brought you in runs back to the airport, so budget about 35 to 45 minutes depending on where you're staying.

🚌 ~35 to 45 min bus + metro to the airport 💵 ~€1.50 🕐 Leave at least 3 hrs before an international flight

💶 Tipping Etiquette

  • Tipping isn't obligatory, but rounding up or adding 10% at restaurants is common and appreciated.
  • Hand the tip directly to the server when paying rather than leaving cash on the table.
  • For cafes and casual bites, rounding to the nearest whole amount is plenty.

👗 Dress Code

  • Churches and synagogues, including St. Vitus Cathedral and the Spanish Synagogue, expect shoulders and knees covered.
  • Smart-casual is fine for most restaurants, and layers matter since Prague's weather can shift quickly across a day.
  • Sturdy, comfortable shoes are essential given the cobblestones on Days 1, 2, and 4.

🚋 Getting Around Prague

  • Prague's tram and metro network (three metro lines plus an extensive tram grid) covers the whole itinerary, and a 90-minute ticket costs about €1.50.
  • A 24-hour pass (~€4.50) is worth it on Days 2 and 3, when you're crossing the city multiple times.
  • The Old Town, Castle, and Josefov are close enough to walk between, so save transit tickets for longer hops like the airport transfer.

⚠️ Safety & Etiquette Warnings

  • Avoid currency-exchange booths advertising "0% commission" near Old Town Square and Wenceslas Square; rates are often far worse than a bank or ATM.
  • Check restaurant menus for prices before ordering in the busiest tourist zones, since some venues near major sights quietly overcharge or add unlisted fees.
  • Pickpocketing is common on crowded trams and around the Astronomical Clock during the hourly show, so keep bags zipped and in front of you.

🗣️ Crucial Phrases in Czech

  • Dobrý den: good day/hello
  • Děkuji: thank you
  • Účet, prosím: the bill, please

💳 Money & Payments

  • The Czech koruna (CZK) is the currency, not the euro, despite the Czech Republic's EU membership, and it's worth having some cash on hand for smaller vendors.
  • Contactless cards are accepted almost everywhere in Prague, including trams, metro machines, and most market stalls.
  • Withdraw koruna from bank-branded ATMs and decline any "dynamic currency conversion" prompt that offers to charge you in your home currency instead.

💰 Money-Saving Hacks

🎟️

Buy the castle circuit ticket, not single entries

The combined ticket covering St. Vitus Cathedral and Golden Lane costs less than paying for each sight separately.

🍺

Eat where locals eat, off the main square

A goulash and pilsner lunch two streets back from Old Town Square can cost half of what the same meal costs on the square itself.

🚋

Get the 24-hour transit pass on cross-city days

On Days 2 and 3, when you're moving between the castle, Josefov, and Malá Strana, the day pass beats paying per ride.

📸 Off-the-Beaten-Path Secrets

🌳

Letná Park instead of the Charles Bridge crowd

A quieter hilltop terrace across the river with a nearly identical view of the bridge and castle skyline, and a fraction of the visitors.

🏰

Vyšehrad over the Castle crowds

A second, far less crowded fortress complex south of the center, with cemetery views and river panoramas that rival the main castle hill.

🕯️

Josefov after the tour buses leave

Late afternoon, once the day-tour groups head back to their buses, the Jewish Quarter's lanes empty out and feel completely different.

✈️ Flights

  • Václav Havel Airport Prague (PRG) is the city's only major airport, about 17 km west of the center.
  • Bus line 119 connects to the Nádraží Veleslavín metro station in about 20 minutes, then it's a short ride into the center on the same ticket.
  • A dedicated Airport Express bus also runs directly to the main train station for travelers heading onward by rail.

🚕 Taxis & Rideshare

  • Bolt and Uber both operate well in Prague and are generally more reliable and transparently priced than hailing a taxi on the street.
  • Street taxis near major tourist sites and the airport have a reputation for overcharging tourists, so booking through an app is safer.
  • The same tourist-zone caution applies here as with currency exchange: confirm the fare or use a metered app before getting in.

🚗 Rental Cars

  • A car isn't needed for this itinerary; every stop from Old Town to Petřín Hill sits on the compact tram and metro network or within walking distance.
  • Prague's Old Town has narrow, partly pedestrianized streets and limited parking, which makes driving downtown more trouble than it's worth.
  • Skip the rental and put the budget toward castle tickets and the river cruise instead.

🏨 Hotels & Where to Stay

  • Old Town (Staré Město): puts Old Town Square, the Astronomical Clock, and Day 1's evening within a five-minute walk.
  • Malá Strana (Lesser Town): a quieter, palace-lined base for Day 2, right at the foot of the castle hill and Charles Bridge.
  • Josefov: a central choice near the Jewish Quarter for Day 3, an easy walk to both Old Town and the riverbank cruise dock.

⛴️ Boat Trips

  • The Vltava river cruise on Day 3 is a genuinely essential part of this itinerary, not a novelty add-on, offering a view of Charles Bridge and the castle hill you can't get on foot.
  • Boats depart regularly from docks along the Old Town riverbank, with no need to book more than a day ahead outside peak summer weekends.
  • Evening dinner cruises are also available for travelers who want to pair the same river view with a full meal.

🧳 Other Essentials

  • Standard Schengen entry rules apply, so non-EU visitors should confirm visa requirements before booking flights.
  • Remember that Czech koruna (CZK), not the euro, is the local currency, and budget a little time to withdraw cash on arrival.
  • Travel insurance is a smart add-on given the pre-booked castle circuit and river-cruise tickets, which are usually non-refundable.
